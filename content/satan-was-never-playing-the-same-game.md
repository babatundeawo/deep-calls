## Some of Us Have Been Telling the Wrong Story

Somewhere between the way we sing about the cross and the way we talk about the fall of man, we accidentally told ourselves a story in which God was surprised. Nobody says that out loud — certainly not in church, where suggesting that God was surprised would earn you an usher and a side-eye from three rows over. But the story is there, buried in the grammar of our theology: man fell, heaven was shaken, and God in His mercy arranged a rescue. The cross, in this telling, is the solution to a problem nobody saw coming. Jesus is Plan B. The atonement is a reaction.

That story is moving. It is also incomplete. And the part that is missing changes everything.

The Bible does not tell the story of a God who scrambled. It tells the story of a God who planned. Not planned in the way a general plans for contingencies — keeping one eye on the battlefield and adjusting as the situation changes. Planned in the way that only omniscience permits: before the battlefield existed, before the soldiers were born, before the first shot was ever fired. The cross was not God's emergency response to Adam's failure. It was God's eternal intention, written into the fabric of creation before creation had a fabric to write on.

This is not a small theological correction. This is the difference between a God who reacts and a God who reigns. Between a God who was outmanoeuvred and had to improvise, and a God whose purposes were never in danger for a single moment. Get this wrong, and you will spend your Christian life living like a refugee from a lost battle. Get it right, and you will understand that you were born into the aftermath of a victory that was settled before time began.

---

## Before Lucifer Cleared His Throat

We must begin where the story actually begins — not in a garden, but in the courts of heaven. The rebellion that broke creation did not originate with a serpent or a piece of fruit. It originated with a creature of extraordinary beauty and intelligence who looked at the throne of God and decided he wanted it.

> *"How you are fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! How you are cut down to the ground, you who weakened the nations! For you have said in your heart: 'I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God; I will also sit on the mount of the congregation on the farthest sides of the north; I will ascend above the heights of the clouds, I will be like the Most High.'"*
> — Isaiah 14:12-14 (NKJV)

Notice the grammar of Lucifer's ambition. Five declarations. Five times the words "I will." The creature had barely opened his mouth before pride had already written his obituary. This is what pride does — it makes a being with finite power speak in the language of infinite intention. I will ascend. I will exalt. I will sit. I will be like the Most High. Every sentence a promissory note the speaker had no currency to honour.

Sin, then, is not a human invention. It is a heavenly import smuggled into creation by a being who was cast out of heaven before Adam ever took his first breath. When we talk about the fall of man as though it were the origin of evil, we have started the story in the second chapter. The war was already in progress. The enemy was already at work. What happened in Eden was not the beginning of the conflict; it was the opening of a second front.

This matters because it reshapes how we read the whole narrative. Adam did not create a problem that God had to solve. Adam became the entry point through which a pre-existing rebellion gained a foothold in the earthly realm. And God, who had watched the rebellion unfold in heaven before it ever touched earth, had already made provision for it — not after the fall, but before the foundations were ever laid.

## The Plan That Predates Every Problem

There is a verse in the letter to the Ephesians that most Christians have read and very few have truly reckoned with. It is not a difficult verse. It is a devastating one.

> *"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ, just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before Him in love, having predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will, to the praise of the glory of His grace, by which He made us accepted in the Beloved. In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins, according to the riches of His grace which He made to abound toward us in all wisdom and prudence, having made known to us the mystery of His will, according to His good pleasure which He purposed in Himself, that in the dispensation of the fullness of the times He might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven and which are on earth in Him. In Him also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestined according to the purpose of Him who works all things according to the counsel of His will, that we who first trusted in Christ should be to the praise of His glory."*
> — Ephesians 1:3-12 (NKJV)

Before the foundation of the world. That phrase should stop the reader cold. Not after the fall. Not following the invention of sin. Not in response to the news that Adam had handed dominion to the enemy. Before the world had a foundation at all, God had already chosen us in Christ. Had already predestined our adoption. Had already purposed to gather all things in heaven and on earth into the headship of His Son. The plan did not emerge from the crisis. The plan preceded the crisis by an eternity.

Revelation 13:8 presses this even further. It speaks of "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world." Not the Lamb who would eventually be slain in response to circumstances. The Lamb whose slaying was written into the eternal counsel of God before the first word of Genesis was ever spoken. We did not stumble into God's plan; God's plan was already in place at the point where we would stumble. The cross was not an improvisation. It was a destination.

Jesus, then, is not heaven's emergency measure. He is eternity's singular intention. To reduce him to a rescue mission is to misread the entire scope of what God was always doing. The rescue is real — profoundly, gloriously real — but it is not the whole picture. God was not merely trying to recover what was lost. God was, and has always been, bringing all of creation to its appointed end: gathered, redeemed, and reconciled under the lordship of His Son.

---

## Yes, God Knew. And That Is the Best News You Have Ever Heard

The question that surfaces here is one that serious people have always asked: if God knew Adam would sin, does that make God responsible for it? Or worse — does it make the whole story a performance? A divine script in which human beings are merely actors reading lines they were given?

This is a real question and it deserves a real answer, not a pastoral brush-off.

God's foreknowledge does not cancel human freedom. The two are not the same thing. A parent who warns their child not to run near the edge of a swimming pool knows, with the kind of knowledge that comes from loving a small and reckless creature, that the child will very likely run anyway. That foreknowledge does not make the child's running any less their own choice, and it does not make the parent responsible for the consequences. What it does — what any thoughtful parent does — is prepare. You warn the child, yes. And then you make sure the first aid kit is stocked and close at hand, because love that only gives instructions without making provision is not love at all.

God warned Adam with the full weight of His authority. "Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die" (Genesis 2:17, NKJV). And then God, who is not a parent fumbling for antiseptic after the accident, had already prepared a provision that made the first aid kit look like an afterthought. The provision was His own Son. The preparation was made before the accident was even possible.

The moment Adam and Eve covered themselves with fig leaves and hid among the trees, God did something remarkable. Before any sentence was pronounced, before the curse was spoken over the ground or the serpent, He said this:

> *"And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your seed and her Seed; He shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise His heel."*
> — Genesis 3:15 (NKJV)

Theologians call this the protoevangelium — the first gospel. The first announcement of the coming Christ, spoken directly to the serpent, before Adam had even fully processed the weight of what he had done. God did not wait for humanity to ask for a saviour. He announced one before humanity had developed the vocabulary to ask. That is not a God who is scrambling. That is a God whose mercy is so far ahead of our failure that it was waiting for us at the point of our fall.

---

## Ludo vs. Chess

Now we must talk about Satan's strategic thinking — and this requires a measure of charity, because the truth is that Lucifer is not stupid. He is, by any reckoning, a creature of extraordinary intelligence, ancient experience, and considerable cunning. The tragedy of his situation is not that he is a fool. It is that he is a brilliant creature playing the wrong game.

In Nigeria, we grow up knowing the game of ludo. It is a game of position and blocking — you watch the board in front of you, you move your pieces, you try to anticipate what your opponent will do in the next turn or two. It is a fine game. It is simply not the game God was playing. God was engaged in something that has no earthly analogy — a game whose board extends across all of eternity, past and future simultaneously visible, every move already known, the outcome never in doubt. Lucifer saw the board in front of him. God saw the end from the beginning.

Consider the five "I will" declarations of Isaiah 14 again. The entire content of Lucifer's rebellion was: I want what is above me. He wanted the throne. He could not get it in heaven — that much was clear once Michael and his angels dealt with him — but then God created a terrestrial domain and placed in it a creature made in His own image, given dominion over the earth. Lucifer looked at Adam and Eve and saw an opportunity. He had always wanted to be "like the Most High." Here were creatures who had been given the image of the Most High. If he could not get God's throne, perhaps he could get God's proxy.

> *"Then the serpent said to the woman, 'You will not surely die. For God knows that in the day you eat of it, your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.'"*
> — Genesis 3:4-5 (NKJV)

The irony of this temptation is almost too rich to hold in one sentence: the creature who fell from heaven because he wanted to be like God was now using that same desire as the lure to bring humanity down. "You will be like God," the serpent said — and the one saying it was the creature whose entire catastrophe had begun with those exact words echoing in his own heart.

Satan succeeded. He gained dominion over the earthly realm. So completely did he succeed that by the time Jesus came, Satan was bold enough to stand on a mountain and offer the kingdoms of the world as a bargaining chip.

> *"Again, the devil took Him up on an exceedingly high mountain and showed Him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory. And he said to Him, 'All these things I will give You if You will fall down and worship me.'"*
> — Matthew 4:8-9 (NKJV)

Stop and sit with the audacity of that. Satan was offering God what belonged to God. He was negotiating with the one who owns "the earth and everything in it" (Psalm 24:1). He was playing ludo with the author of time itself — pointing to pieces on a board that his opponent had already seen the end of. Jesus did not even dignify the offer with a theological argument. He simply said: "Away with you, Satan."

That is not frustration. That is the calm of someone who already knows how the game ends.

---

## The Joker in the Deck

Here is what Satan did not see coming. He did not see it because there was no precedent for it, no category in his ancient intelligence that could have prepared him for it. God Himself would enter creation.

Not as a conqueror arriving in power to take back by force what had been lost. As a baby. In a feeding trough. In the town of Bethlehem, under the rule of an occupying Roman empire, born to parents who had to explain the circumstances of the conception to anyone who cared to ask. The King of eternity arrived in the most defenceless form creation has to offer, and the enemy — brilliant, ancient, cunning — simply did not have a playbook for this move.

> *"But when the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, that we might receive the adoption as sons."*
> — Galatians 4:4-5 (NKJV)

"The fullness of the time." God did not rush. He waited with the patience of someone who is not subject to time at all — waited through the covenants with Noah and Abraham and Moses and David, waited through the prophets and their declarations, waited until every piece was in precisely the right position. And then He came.

The writer of Hebrews explains what this move was designed to accomplish: *"that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil, and release those who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage"* (Hebrews 2:14, NKJV). The genius of the Incarnation is not just that God became human. It is that God became mortal — took on the very condition through which the enemy held his power — in order to demolish that power from the inside.

And John, the beloved disciple, strips it back to its simplest statement: *"For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil"* (1 John 3:8, NKJV). Not to improve them. Not to negotiate with them. To destroy them. The Incarnation was not a pastoral visit. It was an act of war — the most unexpected, most decisive act of war in the history of creation.

---

## He Killed the Wrong Man

This is where the floor shifts.

Satan, working through the religious establishment and the machinery of Roman power, arranged the crucifixion of Jesus Christ. He thought — insofar as one can speak of Satan's thoughts — that this was the finishing move. Kill the Son of God. End the threat. Secure the dominion forever.

He had no idea he was being used.

O Lucifer — you who aspired to wisdom, you who declared yourself fit to sit above the stars of God — did you not read the scroll? Did the words of Isaiah not reach you in whatever realm you occupied when they were written? *"Surely He has borne our griefs and carried our sorrows... He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement for our peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed"* (Isaiah 53:4-5, NKJV). The cross was not your ambush. It was His appointment.

Here is what happened at Calvary, stated plainly: Jesus took upon Himself the entire weight of human sin. Not symbolically. Not representationally in some theological abstraction. He became sin — *"For He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him"* (2 Corinthians 5:21, NKJV) — and then He took sin to the grave. When Jesus died, sin died with Him. Not individual sins, catalogued and archived. Sin itself — the principle, the power, the legal claim it had over humanity — went into the earth with Jesus, and did not come back out.

Here is why that is the end of everything Satan had built. Satan's dominion was legitimate — and this is the hard, honest thing we must say — because it was grounded in a legal reality. The law declared: "The soul who sins shall die" (Ezekiel 18:4, NKJV). Sin created the debt; the law enforced it; death was the penalty. Satan was not a squatter in the earthly realm. He was operating under the terms of a transaction that Adam had signed. That transaction required sin to be valid. Remove sin, and the contract collapses.

> *"O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your victory? The sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin is the law. But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ."*
> — 1 Corinthians 15:55-57 (NKJV)

The sting of death is sin. The strength of sin is the law. When sin was destroyed at Calvary, death was defanged. When grace superseded law at the cross — when the certificate of every legal debt owed by humanity was nailed to that timber and cancelled (Colossians 2:14) — sin lost the very thing that gave it power. Satan had built his entire dominion on the combination of sin and law. Both were demolished on a Friday afternoon outside the walls of Jerusalem, by the very execution he had orchestrated.

He thought he was winning. He had just lost everything.

---

## The Giver of Life Cannot Be Held by Death

But God was not finished.

The first Adam, Scripture tells us, was formed from the dust of the ground, and God breathed into his nostrils the breath of life and he became a living being (Genesis 2:7). A living being, animated by the breath of another. Dependent. Derivative. Beautiful, yes; bearing the image of God, yes — but a creature who received life rather than one who originated it.

The second Adam was something categorically different.

> *"And so it is written, 'The first man Adam became a living being.' The last Adam became a life-giving spirit... The first man was of the earth, made of dust; the second Man is the Lord from heaven."*
> — 1 Corinthians 15:45, 47 (NKJV)

A life-giving spirit. The Lord from heaven. How, then, do you hold such a being in a tomb? How does the grave retain the one who is the origin of life itself? When Jesus stood outside the tomb of Lazarus — who had been dead long enough that his sister warned of the smell — He did not plead with death. He did not petition heaven for a miracle. He declared:

> *"I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in Me, though he may die, he shall live. And whoever lives and believes in Me shall never die."*
> — John 11:25-26 (NKJV)

He said this and then proved it immediately — Lazarus walked out. Three days later, He proved it permanently. Death did not release Jesus because it was persuaded. Death released Jesus because it had no authority over the one who had just destroyed the legal grounds on which death operated. The resurrection is not just a miracle. It is a verdict. It is God's public declaration over everything that transpired at the cross: the debt is cancelled, the enemy is defeated, the victory is complete.

When He came out of that tomb, He came out holding everything.

> *"And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, 'All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.'"*
> — Matthew 28:18 (NKJV)

All authority. Not some authority, managed jointly with the powers that had claimed the earthly realm. Not authority pending appeal. All of it — in heaven, and on earth — gathered into the hands of the risen Christ. Colossians 2 renders the moment in the language of a Roman triumph, the ancient procession in which a victorious general paraded his defeated enemies through the streets of the city for all to see their humiliation:

> *"Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it."*
> — Colossians 2:15 (NKJV)

Not a quiet administrative transfer. A public spectacle. A parade. God, in Christ, walked the defeated powers through the streets of eternity and let every created being see what the cross had accomplished.

In heaven, the response was what you would expect:

> *"After these things I heard a loud voice of a great multitude in heaven, saying, 'Alleluia! Salvation and glory and honour and power belong to the Lord our God!'"*
> — Revelation 19:1 (NKJV)

---

## And He Left You Armed

Here is the thing about this victory that matters most for how you live your life on a Tuesday morning.

Jesus did not ascend and leave humanity without recourse. Before He went, He made sure the people who bear His name understood what they were carrying. He told His disciples: *"Behold, I give you the authority to trample on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you"* (Luke 10:19, NKJV). Not some of the power of the enemy. All of it. And He told the Colossians what their position in Christ actually means:

> *"For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily; and you are complete in Him, who is the head of all principality and power."*
> — Colossians 2:9-10 (NKJV)

Complete. Not almost complete. Not partially complete, pending good behaviour or sufficient prayer hours. Complete in the one who is the head of every power that exists.

Now let us be precise here, because the Nigerian church has not always been. This is not triumphalism. This is not the theology of the preacher who says that if you are sick or struggling, it is because you lack faith, or because your seed sowing has been insufficient, or because you have not yet found the right prophet to decree your breakthrough. That theology is a different kind of lie, and it has done tremendous damage to people who were already hurting. The victory of Christ does not mean the absence of suffering in this age. Paul, who understood the authority of the believer better than almost anyone, also described being "hard pressed on every side, yet not crushed; perplexed, but not in despair; persecuted, but not abandoned; struck down, but not destroyed" (2 Corinthians 4:8-9, NIV). The victory is real. The war is still active. Both things are true.

What the victory does mean is this: you are not a casualty. You are not a victim of spiritual forces that are beyond your reckoning. You are not an accidental participant in a cosmic conflict that was arranged without you in mind. You are, in Christ, a full beneficiary of a war that was won before you were born — and you have been equipped, not merely comforted.

---

## What Will You Do With This?

There are people reading this who have been treating Jesus as a last resort. A spiritual insurance policy, invoked in moments of crisis and largely ignored when life is manageable. There are people who have given more time to consulting those who consult the spirits of ancestors than they have given to the one who, according to this very argument, held those spirits up to public shame at Calvary. There are people who know the name of Jesus the way they know the name of a wealthy relative they have never met — useful in conversation, invoked in prayer, but not genuinely known.

This argument asks more of you than that.

If the cross was not an accident — if Jesus was the plan before Lucifer's pride had a chance to form its first sentence — then the question of what you do with Him is the most serious question your life contains. Not because the answer wins you divine favour in a transaction. Because the answer is a response to something God purposed before time itself began. You are not being asked to join a religion. You are being invited into a story whose ending was written in eternity past, whose central character is the same yesterday, today, and forever, and whose victory has already been declared over every power that has ever held you.

The war is over. The question is whether you will live like a soldier who has received the news, or like one still hiding in the jungle, fighting a conflict that ended decades ago.

Jesus Christ is not a moral teacher who said some interesting things. He is not a cultural figure useful for Nigerian naming ceremonies and Christmas services. He is the reason everything was made, the answer to everything that went wrong, and the one in whom everything in heaven and on earth is being brought together. The scope of who He is will not fit inside the theology of your local prayer mountain or the ambitions of any prosperity preacher who has ever told you that his God is too small to save you without your seed.

He is larger than that. The plan was always larger than that.

And you, if you are willing, are part of it.

## External Sources

- **Augustine of Hippo** — *The City of God* (413-426 AD): Augustine's extended treatment of the two cities — the city of God and the city of man — maps directly onto the cosmic conflict described here. His argument that the fall does not undo the eternal purposes of God informs the theological backbone of this article.
- **C.S. Lewis** — *Mere Christianity* (1952): Lewis's observation that the enemy is "an angel, not a god" — a finite creature of great power operating under the delusion that finite ambition can outlast infinite sovereignty — echoes the chess/ludo analysis of Satan's strategic miscalculation.
- **N.T. Wright** — *Evil and the Justice of God* (2006): Wright's argument that the cross is not merely a legal transaction but a cosmic event in which the powers of evil overreach themselves and are defeated by the very act they intended as victory — forms the theological frame for the section on the cross as the enemy's undoing.
