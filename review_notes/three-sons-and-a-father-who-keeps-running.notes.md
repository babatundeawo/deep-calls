# Notes: three-sons-and-a-father-who-keeps-running
Word count: about 8170 body words (original 5190). Filename kept for URL stability.
Thesis: In Luke 15 a father goes out for both lost sons, the elder brother's problem is a ledger mentality that hides his sonship, and the story's empty place is filled by Christ the brother who goes out at the cost of the cross.
Volta: the section "What the Welcome Costs the Father" (about 45 percent) turns the story to the atonement; the final turn to the third son is at about 85 percent.
Alternative title: A Father, Two Sons and an Empty Chair.

## Restored from the original
Tied versus untied sheep and the colt (held loosely, with caveat); lost coin as non rebellious lostness; "God gives you what you want so you discover what you need"; Esther Diya (love of money, senselessness); Tomilola Adedeji (foolishness flees accountability; famine reveals); 1 Timothy 6:10 (with a correction of the original's "singular article" claim: the Greek has no article); Romans 1:28; Proverbs 18:1; Luke 15:17 Greek; Marvellous Oluwadunsin (working versus serving, zeal without knowledge, failure to mirror the father); "this son of yours" vs my brother; Temiloluwa Adedeji (servants claiming the inheritance); the whole fasting and prayer section (Matthew 17, Matthew 4, Isaiah 40:31); the elder brother as household representative; closing invitation.

## Added
Ezekiel 34 behind the shepherd; the 1 in 100, 1 in 10, 1 in 2 progression; Ben Sira on dividing an estate in life; Deuteronomy 21:17 double portion and 21:18 to 21 rebellious son; Ukeje v Ukeje (2014); prevenient grace across Reformed and Arminian; a full section on the cost to the father and the objection that Luke 15 makes the cross unnecessary (Abelard, Anselm, Aulen, penal substitution, Romans 3:26); Jonah 4 parallel; Myers on the African eldest brother; textual note on Matthew 17:21 and Mark 9:29; caution about prosperity readings of "inheritance"; Galatians 4:7.
